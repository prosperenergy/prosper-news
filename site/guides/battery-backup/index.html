Guide + calculator · Battery storage

What will a home battery actually keep running?

Energy, output power, protected circuits and a calculator that shows its assumptions.

A battery should come with an answer to a simple question: what will it run, and for how long? Not “whole-home peace of mind.” An actual list of loads, an output limit and a duration estimate.

The answer depends on the system, its charge when the outage starts and what you ask it to do. This guide is a planning aid, not a design approval or a guarantee of runtime.

Energy is the tank. Power is the flow.

Kilowatt-hours (kWh) describe energy. Kilowatts (kW) describe how quickly energy can be delivered. A battery can contain enough energy for a task but lack the inverter output to start a large appliance. Motors and compressors can briefly need much more power than their running load.

The protected circuits matter too. Equipment outside the backed-up panel does not receive power just because a battery exists somewhere in the house. DOE storage basics

Estimate a planning ceiling

The calculator below divides available usable energy by an assumed average load. Enter usable AC-deliverable energy, not raw cell capacity. If you only have a DC or nominal specification, ask your installer for the corresponding usable AC value.

It excludes future solar recharging, changing loads, degradation, temperature effects and additional conversion losses. It also does not check continuous output or motor-start capability. Those omissions make this an idealized planning ceiling, not a guaranteed outage duration.

A battery is not automatically an outage system

Most conventional grid-connected solar systems shut down during a utility outage to avoid unsafe backfeeding. Backup requires suitable equipment, controls and safe separation from the grid. Some system configurations offer limited solar-only backup, but you should never assume yours does. DOE rooftop resilience

Ask for the specific operating sequence: grid fails, equipment disconnects safely, backed-up circuits stay energized, and solar either can or cannot recharge the battery. Ask how it restarts after the battery empties.

Make a load list before buying

Put necessities first: refrigeration, selected lights, communications and other loads your family identifies as important. Then add the equipment-specific running watts, startup requirements and estimated hours of use. Heating, cooling, well pumps and electric cooking can change the design substantially.

For critical medical equipment, work from its actual requirements and a clinician/equipment-provider-informed emergency plan. A generic battery estimate is not an adequate sole life-safety plan.

Safety belongs in the proposal

Ask for the exact model, applicable system certification, installation instructions, required clearances, location, permitting and inspection. UL 9540 addresses energy-storage system safety; UL 9540A is a thermal-runaway fire-propagation test method. A test report is not permission to ignore local code or manufacturer instructions. UL safety guidance

Do not open, modify or improvise wiring to a home battery. Faults, damage, unusual heat or odors warrant professional guidance; follow emergency-service instructions where there is an immediate hazard.

Check the economics separately

Backup value, bill savings and grid-service compensation are three different benefits. A backup reserve may limit the energy available for daily bill management. Grid participation may add cycling. Tariffs, export rules and contract terms decide whether a predicted benefit reaches your wallet.

Request a complete installed cash price and, if financed, the full payment schedule and total of payments. Compare the bill with and without the battery under the same usage and tariff assumptions. Do not treat a tax benefit or program payment as guaranteed without establishing current eligibility.
