A LETTER FROM CRAIG STRATTON

Be the one
they follow.

From a first rooftop panel in 2007 to the next chapter in home energy, Craig Stratton shares what it takes to earn trust, build leaders, and create something people can count on.

ByCraig StrattonFounder, Prosper
October 4, 202611 min read

“The promise still has to work when you are no longer standing there to explain it.”

I installed my very first solar panel on a roof on February 2, 2007.

I still come back to that date. Everything we have built since then carries a connection to that beginning: getting close to the work, learning how it really works, and being willing to keep going.

I was early in bringing home automation, alarms, and solar together. I could see an opportunity in connecting those pieces. I’ve been doubted. I know that being early can mean seeing something clearly before the people around you are ready to see it.

You still have to do the work.

That is the part I want to share with you. The learning. The problems you have to solve. The patience it takes to keep improving when the excitement wears off and the hard part is still in front of you.

Prosper Security grew to more than 17,000 accounts. I later sold that company for about $10 million. Then I created Prosper Solar, and we went on to complete more than 25,000 installations across the United States.

Those are meaningful milestones. What I want you to carry from them is the possibility of building something substantial from a decision to begin, followed by a willingness to stay with the work.

Expertise grows there. In the questions you finally learn how to answer. In the problem you can now help someone else solve. In the experience that lets you look a customer in the eye and explain what will happen next.

Eventually, people begin to count on you.

That means something to me. It is at the heart of what I want Prosper to be, and what I want this opportunity to become for you.

Build something people can count on.

BRING THE BIG CONVERSATION HOME

We talk about energy in enormous numbers. Generation. Infrastructure. Utility transactions worth billions. It can feel so large that the person living in the house gets lost in the conversation.

I want to bring it back to that person.

A homeowner wants to understand the bill. They want to know what happens when the power goes out. They want to make a good decision with the money they have and feel confident that the person helping them knows what they’re talking about.

Those are reasonable things to want. Helping someone get there is work worth doing.

The national energy challenge has several parts. We need power available when people need it, and we need a system that can deliver it. High-voltage transmission moves electricity across regions. Local lines, substations, and transformers bring it to the house. A problem at any of those levels can affect reliability.

Adding generation alone will not fix every delivery bottleneck. Equipment also has to stay within safe limits. Some aging infrastructure needs replacement; other parts need upgrades that let power move more effectively.

That work deserves serious attention. Households also deserve choices they can evaluate while those larger projects move forward.

The utility landscape keeps changing, too. Dominion’s proposed multibillion-dollar combination with Florida-based NextEra Energy, still subject to approvals, is one example. Decisions at that scale can feel a long way from an individual homeowner’s choices.

I understand wanting more say in your own energy future. I want people to have practical ways to gain it.

Questions about major energy projects, including nuclear power, deserve clear, evidence-based answers about health, safety, monitoring, and emergency planning. Whatever direction those larger debates take, there is a useful question we can help a homeowner answer today:

What would give this home more control over the power it produces, uses, and has available when it matters?

A PROMISE THAT WORKS AFTER YOU LEAVE

Solar and storage give us a way to have that conversation in practical terms.

Solar produces electricity at the home and can reduce the power purchased from the grid. A battery can store energy for later. A properly designed system can help a household use more of its own solar and keep selected loads running during an outage.

That is a meaningful benefit. It deserves to be explained carefully.

Most ordinary grid-connected solar systems shut down when the grid goes out. Backup requires equipment and controls designed to operate safely during an outage. Battery capacity, inverter limits, available charge, household demand, and the length of an outage all shape what will actually run and for how long.

A customer should understand that before making the decision.

I want our reps to care about what happens after the conversation is over. After the paperwork. After the installation. The promise still has to work when you are no longer standing there to explain it.

If a homeowner wants backup, help them understand what will be backed up. If they want to use more of their own solar, explain how storage could help. If a proposal does not fit the home, say so. The customer is trusting you to help them make a good decision.

That is how trust becomes a referral. Someone feels they were heard, the explanation made sense, and the result matched what they were told.

For homes that already have solar, I believe the next conversation should include storage. Understand what the homeowner has, what they need, and whether a battery adds enough value to justify the cost.

For a new system, think about generation, storage, and backup together. Give the homeowner a clear picture of the choices.

More control over your home is worth pursuing. We earn the right to help people pursue it by being honest about what a system can do.

Build something people can count on.

GIVE PEOPLE ROOM TO MAKE A GOOD DECISION

Life gives people plenty to deal with. An energy decision can stay on the list until a surprise bill or an outage makes it urgent.

I want us to help people earlier, while there is time to think.

Planning gives a homeowner room to understand their usage, decide what matters most, compare equipment, and work through the cost. It lets them ask the second question and the third. They deserve that time.

Utility rules belong in that conversation. Net-metering and solar export-credit policies vary by state, utility, and tariff. Some markets are changing how exported electricity is valued; others continue net metering, sometimes with different rules for new and existing customers.

Know the rules that apply to the actual address. Understand any eligibility requirements and deadlines. Give people a savings estimate built around their situation.

Prices and financing terms can change. Planning now lets a household compare real options before circumstances narrow them. There is enough genuine value in preparation to make a strong case for it.

My preference is ownership when the economics fit. I like the idea of a homeowner building something they control over the long term.

That conviction comes with a responsibility to look at the full agreement. Put the cash price beside the total financed cost. Understand the fees, warranties, and maintenance responsibilities.

A lease or power-purchase agreement may lower the upfront barrier, and ownership remains with someone else. Read the full term, any annual increases, and what happens if the home is sold or the customer wants to buy out the agreement.

An agreement can remain in someone’s life for years. Help them understand it before they sign.

That level of care is part of building a lasting business. People remember whether you helped them make a decision they could feel good about.

EARN THE PLACE YOU WANT TO LEAD FROM

Now I want to talk directly to the reps reading this.

There is room here to do more than close the next deal. There is room to become someone a customer refers without hesitation, someone another rep calls for a real answer, someone the company trusts with greater responsibility.

I want you to see that possibility in yourself.

Early in a market, a lot still has to be learned. Customers have questions. Projects run into complications. New reps need guidance. Each of those is a chance to become more useful by doing the work.

The knowledge you build now can become the reason people choose to work with you later.

Income matters. I want you to have the opportunity to build more of it. Strong commission opportunities deserve attention, and competition can put pressure on payouts as a market develops. Use this period to build the expertise and reputation that can serve you as the market changes.

Learn enough to give a clear answer. Follow through when you say you will. Own the part of a problem you can solve. Get help with the part you cannot. Then share what you learned.

That last part matters.

The next rep may be trying to understand something you struggled with at the beginning. Take the time to explain it. Help them build judgment. Give them something solid to carry into their next conversation.

That is leadership taking shape.

If you want a team, show that you can be trusted with one. Show that you can produce and that you can help other people get better. When the company is deciding who can lead more reps, give it a clear answer through the way you work.

Being trusted with people is a serious responsibility. Their experience will be shaped by what you teach, what you expect, and how you respond when something gets difficult.

A team can feel the difference between being recruited and being led.

Leadership can open the possibility of greater responsibility and income tied to more than your own individual production. It also asks more of you. You have to care about whether the people beside you are actually succeeding.

That is the kind of growth I want to build at Prosper. Reps becoming experts. Experts becoming leaders. Leaders helping other people find their footing and build something of their own within the team.

When people follow you because they trust your judgment and know you will show up for them, you have earned something worth protecting.

STAY LONG ENOUGH TO BECOME SOMEONE THEY COUNT ON

Being doubted can wear on you. So can being new at something you want to be good at.

Keep learning. Keep asking. Change your approach when the work shows you a better way. Let each hard part leave you more capable than it found you.

That is what I mean when I say stick it out.

Give yourself the chance to become the experienced person you are working toward. The one with useful answers. The one who can steady a new rep, explain a difficult choice, and help a team move forward.

The OGs get shit done.

They pave the way. They learn enough to help the next person. They earn trust, then keep earning it.

I want us to carry that spirit into this next chapter of home energy. Real knowledge. Honest promises. Work that helps people feel more confident about their homes and their futures.

Live fearlessly by getting informed, making a plan, and taking the next useful step. Give yourself permission to begin before you have every answer, and take responsibility for finding the answers you need.

Be good at what you do. Be different in a way people can feel. Leave them more informed, more capable, and more confident than you found them.

On February 2, 2007, I installed my first panel.

Today, I am asking you to think about what you can build from the place where you are standing.

Learn the business. Take care of the customer. Help the next rep. Build the team.

And keep going until your experience becomes something another person can lean on.

Build something people can count on.

Become the one they follow.

— Craig Stratton
Founder, Prosper
