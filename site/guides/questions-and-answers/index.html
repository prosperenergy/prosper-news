FAQ + glossary · Working solar

The questions people ask at the kitchen table.

Direct answers on nighttime power, outages, SMRs, savings and the words used to sell energy.

These are the questions that matter when the discussion moves from national headlines to a family's roof, garage and electric bill.

Are small modular reactors all talk?

No. Operating examples exist in Russia and China, and Darlington is a real construction program. What remains to be demonstrated for a specific proposed project is its completed cost, delivery, operating performance and customer value. IAEA examples · Darlington

Our challenge is not “nuclear is imaginary.” It is “a future reactor is not a reason to put off fixing an available household option.” See the project scorecard.

Could nuclear and solar both be useful?

Yes. Nuclear can supply low-carbon generation; solar, storage, flexible demand and networks can serve other parts of the system. A broad energy portfolio does not decide whether a particular homeowner should wait. Our priority is working solar and appropriate storage that can be evaluated now. IEA nuclear analysis · DOE storage guidance

Does solar work at night?

Panels need light to generate. A battery can carry previously stored energy into later hours, within its available energy and output limits. The grid or another properly designed supply may still be needed. “Solar plus storage” is not a promise of unlimited nighttime power. Storage basics

Will my solar work during an outage?

Not necessarily. Standard grid-tied systems commonly disconnect when utility service fails. Backup requires suitable equipment and controls. Ask about your exact system, protected circuits and restart behavior. DOE resilience guide

Why am I still getting an electric bill?

Possible reasons include fixed charges, nighttime purchases, higher household use, seasonal production, export rules or a system fault. A bill alone does not diagnose the cause. Compare monitoring, usage, tariff and equipment records. Use the performance checklist.

Does a battery fix underperforming solar?

No. It can store available electricity; it does not repair failed generating equipment. Diagnose production and account problems first, then assess whether storage helps the remaining need. Solar service checklist.

Is a battery always cheaper than buying grid power?

No. Installed cost, financing, tariff, export value, usage, losses and lifetime all matter. Backup may have value even without a short bill-savings payback, but the proposal should say so clearly. Compare the complete costs.

Can a battery power my whole home?

Sometimes a properly sized and configured system can support a broad set of loads. The term alone does not specify duration or startup capability. Ask for the backed-up circuit list, usable energy, continuous output and surge capability. Backup guide and calculator.

Can I get paid for helping the grid?

Some programs compensate eligible devices for grid services. Availability, enrollment, dispatch and payment terms vary. Do not assume eligibility or guaranteed revenue. Read the agreement and protect your backup reserve. Power-control checklist.

Would half of solar homes adding batteries replace nuclear?

Not one for one. Our baseline scenario adds about 39.3 GWh of storage, not a new source of generation. It can serve limited-duration needs if the systems are charged and available. The cost and rollout are explicit assumptions. Run the national scenario.

Does living near nuclear prove a cancer risk increase?

Proximity alone does not establish an individual's exposure or prove causation. Some studies report concerning associations; other studies do not show elevated rates in the populations they examined. Radiation risks, accidents and routine operation require careful distinctions. Read the health evidence, including contrary findings.

Are solar and batteries risk-free?

No. Electrical safety, battery fire risk, manufacturing, mining, recycling and responsible disposal all matter. Use qualified professionals, appropriate equipment and a real service plan. Lifecycle guide · UL safety guidance.

What do these terms mean?

kW / MW / GW: units of power, or rate of energy delivery. One GW is 1,000 MW; one MW is 1,000 kW.

kWh / MWh / GWh: units of energy. One kW sustained for one hour is one kWh.

SMR: small modular reactor; a category with different designs and development stages, not a single standardized product.

LCOE: levelized cost of energy, a modeled lifetime generation-cost measure. It is not automatically a customer tariff or an all-hour reliability price.

FOAK / NOAK: first-of-a-kind versus nth-of-a-kind. A first project and an assumed mature production run can have very different economics.

PTO: permission to operate, generally part of the utility process for a grid-connected system; requirements vary.

VPP: virtual power plant, an aggregation of distributed devices coordinated to deliver services.

Backup reserve: stored energy held back from other uses to support outage needs, subject to the actual equipment and agreement.

Where should I start?

Already have solar? Check that it is working. Considering storage? Build your backup load list. Debating mini nuclear? Use the project records. Want the originals? Open the source library.
