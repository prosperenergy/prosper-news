# Your roof makes power. Who gets to control it?

Prosper News · Updated September 15, 2026

For years, the pitch was about the panel. How much it produced. What it cost. What it might save.

The next argument is about something bigger: **who decides when electricity is used, stored or exported—and who gets paid.**

Solar remains the generation layer. Batteries and software add control. That is the shift homeowners should understand before signing the next agreement.

## A power network can be built from homes

A virtual power plant, or VPP, coordinates distributed devices to provide grid services. Those devices can include batteries, electric vehicles, thermostats and flexible commercial loads. It is a real operating model, not a literal new power station in one place. [DOE VPP guidance](https://www.energy.gov/edf/virtual-power-plants-projects)

A 2025 California event illustrates the idea: a Brattle analysis reported about 535 MW average delivery for two hours from a coordinated residential-battery fleet, using operator-provided telemetry. It is evidence of a specific dispatch event, not proof that every home can participate or that output is available around the clock. [Test-event report](https://www.brattle.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/Assessing-VPP-Performance-Impacts-of-a-Test-Event-in-California-1.pdf)

## Why companies want the battery relationship

Here is our business interpretation: installing panels can start a customer relationship, while storage and dispatch agreements can extend that relationship into recurring energy services. A company with financing, software and a large customer base may seek value from coordinating those systems over time.

That does not make every leasing company a utility. Aggregating devices, selling retail electricity and owning physical distribution wires are distinct activities with different rules. A company can participate in one without doing all three.

The scale is worth noticing. IEA puts global household electricity spending above US$1 trillion in 2024. That establishes a very large electricity economy. It does not establish a trillion-dollar addressable market for VPP operators, or any particular company's future revenue. [IEA expenditure analysis](https://www.iea.org/reports/household-energy-affordability/executive-summary)

## Control is a contract question

The logo on the battery will not answer these questions. The actual ownership, financing, software and grid-services agreements should.

| Ask this | Why it matters |
| --- | --- |
| Who owns the equipment? | A cash purchase, loan, lease and power-purchase agreement can create different rights and obligations. |
| Who can change charging and export settings? | Dispatch control affects household use, available backup and grid participation. |
| What minimum backup reserve is protected? | A payment for grid services is less attractive if it undermines the protection you bought. |
| Can I opt out of an event? | Ask about emergency overrides, notice and any loss of compensation. |
| How is compensation calculated? | Capacity, actual delivery and performance-based payments are different. Ask about fees and payment timing. |
| Who bears additional cycling or degradation? | Check warranty conditions and the responsibility for performance loss. |
| What data is collected and shared? | Ask who can access usage data and what happens when you leave. |
| How do I exit or sell my home? | Transfer, removal, early-termination and software-access terms can matter years later. |

Do not assume an incentive is available just because the hardware supports aggregation. Programs, tariffs, enrollment limits and equipment eligibility vary. Obtain the terms for your utility and location.

## What Prosper should be judged on

Our position is homeowner-first: get the existing system working, design storage around actual needs and make control rights understandable before anyone signs.

This article does **not** claim that Prosper currently operates a VPP, holds a utility distribution franchise or offers a guaranteed grid-services payment. Any specific program must be established in a property-level proposal and agreement.

## The line worth keeping

> Solar makes the power. Storage makes some of it available when you need it. The agreement decides who benefits.

Do not let a discussion about future nuclear projects distract from ownership of the energy assets already sitting on American homes. And do not let enthusiasm for batteries become a reason to skip the contract.
