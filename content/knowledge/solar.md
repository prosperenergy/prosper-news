You are still making the payment. The system should still be doing its job.

Before a new battery proposal, before a debate about mini nuclear, before anyone promises another upgrade: **find out whether the solar you already own is producing as expected.** A battery can move available energy. It cannot repair an inverter, reconnect a failed string or resolve an account that was never properly commissioned.

## Start with three things

Bring your latest electric bill, access to your production app and your original system documents. Twelve months of bills and production are better if you have them. Keep account numbers and personal details private when sharing screenshots publicly.

Then ask three separate questions:

1. Is the system generating electricity?
2. Is that production reasonable for this system and season?
3. Is the utility billing and crediting the account under the correct arrangement?

Those questions are related, but they are not the same. A monitoring outage can hide working equipment. A working array can still produce a disappointing bill if household use rises or export compensation changes.

## The homeowner checklist

| Check | What to collect | What to ask |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Monitoring | Last successful update, daily graph, recent fault alerts | Is the app offline or is the equipment offline? |
| Production | Monthly kWh, year-to-date total, original design estimate | Is the comparison adjusted for season, weather and system age? |
| Equipment | Inverter model, panel count, warranty paperwork | Are there persistent faults, failed components or open service cases? |
| Site changes | New shade, roof work, visible damage observed from the ground | Does a qualified technician need to inspect the array? |
| Utility account | Rate plan, import/export kWh, fixed charges, permission to operate | Are the meter, tariff and credits set up correctly? |
| Household use | New EV, pool, heat pump, occupants or usage schedule | Is the system underperforming, or is the home using more? |
| Service responsibility | Installer, manufacturer and finance/lease contacts | Who diagnoses, who pays, and what does the warranty actually cover? |

Do not climb onto a roof, open electrical equipment or bypass a shutoff to perform this review. Document what you can safely observe and use qualified service personnel.

## Do not diagnose production from one cloudy day

A daily reading is a clue, not a complete performance audit. Compare periods that make sense and separate expected design production from a contractual guarantee. Ask the service team to explain weather, shading, clipping, downtime and equipment degradation where relevant.

If the system has never been approved for grid operation, resolve that process. Local permits, inspection and utility requirements still apply; a new device does not make those obligations disappear. [DOE permitting guidance](source:permits)

## Ask for a written service result

“We looked at it” is not enough. Request the fault found, evidence, proposed repair, responsibility for cost, expected completion date and how restored production will be verified. Keep the case number and the before-and-after readings.

If you have a lease or power-purchase agreement, review the actual service obligations before paying a separate contractor. If your installer is unavailable, the equipment manufacturer may have a service pathway; availability and warranty terms vary.

## Then decide whether storage helps

With performance understood, a battery evaluation can focus on the right problem: outage coverage, time-of-use rates, export value or participation in a local grid-services program.

Solar generates. Storage shifts. Controls determine how they work together. Ask about inverter compatibility, retrofit work, backup circuits, charging rules and utility approval before accepting a price. [DOE solar and storage basics](source:storage-basics)

If your goal is resilience, verify that the system can safely operate when the grid is down. Ordinary grid-tied solar does not automatically provide outage power. [DOE resilience guidance](source:backup)
