An announcement is not a power plant. A licence is not a construction finish. And a model's cheapest future outcome is not a price a customer has paid.

Those distinctions are the backbone of a serious challenge to the mini-nuclear sales pitch. Here are the projects to put on the table, with unlike technologies kept separate.

## The project scorecard

| Project | What is real | Cost or timing evidence | What it does not prove |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Vogtle Units 3 and 4, Georgia | Two completed large reactors, operating since 2023 and 2024 | EIA: about $14B initially, more than $30B ultimately; originally expected in 2016 and 2017 | Not an SMR cost result |
| NuScale / UAMPS, Idaho | Development cancelled in November 2023 | $58/MWh earlier target; $89/MWh January 2023 target; planned first module 2029 | Neither target is an operating electricity price |
| Darlington, Ontario | Licensed construction program with a real site and investment | C$7.7B first unit plus shared infrastructure; C$20.9B four-unit program; first grid connection targeted end-2030 | No completed operating-cost result yet |
| V.C. Summer Units 2 and 3, South Carolina | Unfinished large reactors; completion feasibility work under way | Abandoned in 2017; updated Santee Cooper / Brookfield feasibility milestones in 2026 | A study is not a construction restart or delivered power |

## What has actually operated?

SMRs are not imaginary. The IAEA documents Russia's Akademik Lomonosov floating plant entering commercial operation in 2020 and China's HTR-PM in 2023. These are examples of operating designs, not an exhaustive current global inventory. [IAEA assessment](source:smr-operating)

This matters because “none exists” is an easy claim to disprove. The better question is whether the **specific design, country, supply chain and financing package being proposed** have demonstrated repeatable delivery at the quoted price.

A working reactor overseas cannot establish what a new project will cost in Ontario or the United States. Nor does the label “SMR” mean all designs use the same fuel, cooling, manufacturing process or regulatory pathway.

## Darlington: serious project, unfinished test

Darlington deserves scrutiny precisely because it is beyond a concept drawing. Canada's nuclear regulator issued a construction licence in April 2025, and its project record tracks construction oversight and the operating-licence application. Those are separate stages. [CNSC record](source:cnsc)

The budget breakdown also matters. C$6.1 billion for the first reactor plus C$1.6 billion in shared infrastructure equals C$7.7 billion. Loading all common infrastructure onto one unit and comparing it with a later repeat-unit estimate can distort the result. So can comparing Canadian dollars with U.S. dollars or financed capital with overnight construction costs. [OPG project page](source:darlington)

Here is the fair challenge: **show the completed cost, achieved construction schedule, reliable output and customer price when they exist.** Until then, describe them as budgets and targets.

## The “data centers need firm power” argument

They do need dependable supply. That is a reason to evaluate firm low-carbon generation, demand flexibility, storage and networks together. It is not proof that any particular unbuilt reactor will be cheap or on time.

Meta's January 2026 announcement covers up to 6.6 GW by 2035 across both new and existing nuclear. Existing-plant support and proposed advanced reactors should not be rolled into one “SMRs are already delivering” headline. [Purchaser announcement](source:meta)

An industrial procurement team may rationally make long-term bets. A homeowner deciding whether to repair an inverter or add backup has a different decision and a different timeline.

## Seven questions that make the pitch answerable

1. **Which exact project?** Ask for the site, design, owner and regulator record.
2. **Which milestone?** Design approval, site work, nuclear construction, first power and commercial operation are different.
3. **Which dollars?** Currency, price year, financing, escalation and contingencies must be stated.
4. **Which price?** Is it a target, a signed contract, a modeled LCOE or an observed operating result?
5. **Who carries overruns?** Developer equity, utility customers, taxpayers or purchasers?
6. **How much repetition is assumed?** First-of-a-kind and mature-fleet economics are not interchangeable.
7. **Why should a household wait?** Ask what the project changes about that home's bill, solar production or outage exposure today.

## The takeaway you can use

> Show me the delivered project, not just the projected price. If you want my family to wait, show me how that waiting improves our actual options.

We do not need to deny nuclear's low-carbon output to question new-build execution. We need to stop treating its best-case future as a reason to neglect working solar and well-designed storage now.
